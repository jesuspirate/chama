Two "Copy Browse diagnostics" exports, 25 seconds apart, both
`us-blf · USD · scope all · category all · search "" · Mine off ·

## Every difference, explained

1. The six A-listings B sees and A doesn't: A's export marks all six
   `mine`. Browse → All excludes the viewer's own listings; they live
   under the Mine chip. That is the whole "totally different Browse":
   on a relay where nearly every live listing belongs to one of the two
   testers, each tester sees mostly the other's.
2. The one B-listing A sees and B doesn't: `mine` on B's side. Same rule.
3. `sm_munm05st_76hyjw9n`: B excludes it with reason `currency` — one
   of A's five GBF listings is not USD. Correct per 6.4.16 E.
4. Everything else (≈300 ids) is excluded on both sides, as `hidden`,
   `cancelled@…` or `not-fetched`. No listing is shown on one side and
   `hidden`/`cancelled` on the other. The caches differ in what they
   never fetched, but nothing unfetched on one side is live on the
   other.

Conclusion: Browse parity holds. The two views differ only by Mine and
by one currency mismatch. No fix to the listing pipeline is justified.
The fix is to the question: "All" means "everyone else's", and nothing
on screen says so.

## What the export must say better

- `hidden` is too coarse. Two `hidden` ids have a presence deadline in
  the future (`sm_munlobst_5i2onxys`, the live 3,000-sat Market trade;
  `sm_muoy5oac_0s0feitu`, created ten minutes before the export).
  Those are not "hidden", they are `status:LOCKED` / `status:JOINED`
  or `currency:EUR`. Vocabulary: `mine`, `status:<EscrowStatus>`,
  `presence-lapsed@<ts>`, `cancelled@<ts>`, `other-community` (local
  scope only), `currency:<code>`, `search`, `not-fetched`.
- `reasons` mixes why-in and why-out. Split into `in: [...]` and
  `out: [...]`, or prefix (`+community`, `-currency`).
- Add a one-line summary at the top: `shown N · mine M · other-currency
  K · lapsed L · cancelled C · unfetched U`, so the next diff is one
  line, not 600.

Under the Show row when All is selected: "Everyone's offers but yours
— yours are under Mine." One sentence, muted, and the question never
comes back.
