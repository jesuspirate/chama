# Brief — on-chain first: make the Taproot escrow the path that survives an outage

**Scope of this pass: Part 1 (1a–1d) plus the Part 3 harness for it. Stop and
report before starting Part 2.** Jet's decisions are recorded in §2b and §1a.

Jet, 2026-09-24: with the Gbot Lightning gateways down, Chama is close to
unusable — most people won't use ecash, and on-chain outside the federation is
the only path that doesn't depend on anyone's gateway. Verified against
`6d78603c` (v6.4.8).

## 0. What already exists — correcting the record

Chama **already has a direct, non-Fedimint on-chain escrow**, live on mainnet
since commit `c765083c` (2026-08-14, `ESCROW_NETWORK = MAINNET`,
`src/bond-multisig/onchain-escrow.ts:70`). It is **not** the tranche/slicing
work: slicing is an *ecash* feature and is what `TRADE_SLICING_ENABLED`
pauses. The on-chain escrow ("Tier 2.1") is separate and unpaused, opt-in in
the classic create form for trades of at least `ONCHAIN_ESCROW_THRESHOLD_SATS
= 100_000` (`onchain-escrow.ts:99`, `CreateForm.tsx:2763`).

What it is:

- **Output:** Taproot, NUMS internal key (no key path), three leaves
  (`onchain-escrow.ts:185-243`): COOP 2-of-2 {buyer, seller}; DISPUTE
  `144 CSV` then 2-of-3 {buyer, seller, arbiter}; REFUND
  `<height> CLTV <funder> CHECKSIG`, height = tip + 4,320 (~30 days).
- **Keys:** per-trade BIP86 keys from Chama's seed,
  `m/86'/coin'/1'/0/FNV1a(escrowId)` (`onchain-escrow-funding.ts:44-83`),
  published in CREATE/JOIN as `escrowXonly`, stored by the reducer in
  `escrowKeys`.
- **Funding:** from any external wallet to an address every client rebuilds
  itself; the funder taps "I have sent it", which checks 1 confirmation via
  Esplora (mempool.space, blockstream fallback) and publishes LOCK.
- **Settlement:** PSBTs signed at settlement, NIP-44 over kind 38114,
  verified by a pre-sign checklist (`onchain-escrow-settle.ts:260-335`), broadcast
  by whoever finalises; the winner then sweeps from the per-trade key to their
  own wallet (`onchain-payout-wallet.ts`).
- **Tests:** offline sign/finalise round trips for COOP and DISPUTE in
  `src/escrow-engine/tests.ts`. A signet run reached SETTLED on 2026-08-01.
  **No mainnet escrow trade is recorded anywhere.**

So this brief is not "build on-chain". It's "make the on-chain escrow safe
enough to promote, then reachable, then cheap."

## Part 1 — money safety. Blocks everything else.

### 1a. The funding address can change under the funder (verified)

Before LOCK, the refund height comes from each client's **own** chain tip:
`refundLockUntil: state.lock.onchain?.refundLockUntil ??
onchainRefundHeightRef.current` (`useEscrow.ts:2894`), where the ref is
`tip + REFUND_CLTV_BLOCKS` read in an effect keyed on `state.connected`
(`:2186-2194`). The refund height is inside a leaf, so it changes the Taproot
tree and therefore the address. Consequences:

- A relay reconnect or a reload after a new block re-reads the tip, and the
  next render shows the funder a **different address**. A funder who sent to
  the first one then taps "I have sent it" and the check looks at the second.
- Buyer, seller and arbiter each compute a different pre-lock address and
  can't cross-check the funder's.
- The first address is recoverable only by someone who knows the exact height
  used, which is persisted nowhere.

**Fix:** the refund height is chosen once, by the funder's client, and
**published in a signed event before the address is ever shown** — a small
"funding terms" payload (or a field on the funder's JOIN/CREATE, whichever
fits the reducer better). Every client, including the funder's on another
device, derives the address from that stamped value. LOCK must carry the same
value, and the reducer rejects a LOCK whose height differs from the stamped
one. Never derive an address from a live tip again.

No mainnet on-chain trade has ever been made (Jet, 2026-09-24), so there is
nothing in flight to preserve. The fix can change how the address is derived
without a migration for existing funded escrows. Signet/test fixtures that
depend on the old derivation should be updated, not special-cased.

### 1b. Nobody checks that LOCK's keys are the parties' keys (verify, then fix)

The reducer stores each party's own `escrowXonly` in `escrowKeys`
(`state-machine.ts:427, 741`), but it never compares LOCK's
`buyerXonly`/`sellerXonly`/`arbiterXonly` against them, and never rebuilds the
address at LOCK (only the parser shape-checks terms). And no client verifies
funding on behalf of the **non-funder**: `findBondFundingUtxos` for escrows is
called only by the funder before LOCK (`useEscrow.ts:2909`) and at settlement
(`:2983`). The non-funder's panel moves to "locked" on `lockedAt` alone
(`onchain-escrow-view.ts`).

If that holds, a modified funder client could publish LOCK terms naming a
second key of its own in the counterparty's seat, fund that address, and — once
the counterparty has sent fiat against "locked" — spend through COOP alone.
**Prove or disprove this with a test first.** Then, whichever way it lands:

1. The reducer rejects LOCK unless its buyer/seller keys equal `escrowKeys`
   and its arbiter key is that arbiter's published escrow key or verified bond
   key, and the address rebuilt from those terms matches.
2. Before a non-funder is told the trade is locked — and before any "pay now"
   copy — their client rebuilds the address from the keys each party
   published **themselves** plus the stamped refund height, and confirms on
   chain that it holds at least the amount with the required confirmations.
   Until then the screen says "Checking the deposit on the blockchain…", not
   "locked".

### 1c. An auto-seated arbiter can't sign a dispute (verified)

For an arbiter who never JOINs, the address uses their **bond** key
(`useEscrow.ts:2869-2886`, from `m/86'/coin'/0'/0/index`). But signing always
derives the **escrow** key (`myEscrowKey`, `:2196`, path `…/1'/0/…`) and then
refuses because it doesn't match (`signingKeyMatchesRole`, `:3191-3195`).
`deriveBondSigningKey` is never called in the settlement flow. So in a dispute
on such a trade the arbiter cannot sign, the DISPUTE leaf can't be completed,
and — with 1d — the funds wait out the 30-day CLTV with no in-app way out.

**Fix:** when the committed arbiter key is a bond key, sign with
`deriveBondSigningKey(seed, { index })`, taking the index from the commitment
store (which already maps xonly → keyIndex, `commitment-store.ts:296`). Test a
full DISPUTE round trip with an auto-seated arbiter.

### 1d. The refund leaf exists but nothing can spend it

`refundWitnessFor` has no callers, the settlement `leaf` type is only
`"coop" | "arbiter"` (`types.ts:864`), and `verifySettlementPsbt` accepts only
those. Add a funder-only REFUND flow: after the CLTV height, the funder's
client builds, verifies (single output to the funder's own address, sequence
and locktime right) and broadcasts the refund. Surface it on the trade from the
height onward. This is the funder's last resort; it must exist before we ask
anyone to fund on-chain in larger numbers.

## Part 2 — make it reachable when Lightning is down

### 2a. In the guided flow

On-chain can be chosen only in the classic create form. Guided results
already group on-chain listings (`groupBySettlementRail`), but posting an
offer from the guided flow can't choose it. Add the rail to the guided post
path, with the checkout below, and let the funding card of an on-chain trade
open straight on the on-chain panel.

### 2b. The threshold is a fee decision — show the fee, let people choose

The 100k floor exists because fees are real: settlement is 162 vB (COOP,
measured, `onchain-escrow.ts:402-409`) plus a ~111 vB sweep today — roughly
1,400 sats at 5 sat/vB and 5,500 at 20 sat/vB, plus the funder's own send.
The code's own note: 1.7% at 100k, 0.35% at 500k, "absurd below 50k".

**Decided by Jet, 2026-09-24:** keep 100k as the **default suggestion**, and
allow on-chain from a hard floor of **25,000 sats**, with a checkout in the same
right-aligned rows as the Fedimint on-chain card — trade, estimated network
fees at the current rate, and fee as a percentage — red when the percentage is
high. With 2d the settlement cost roughly halves, which is what makes a lower
floor honest.

### 2c. Funding that feels like a payment

The panel shows a plain address with Copy (`OnchainEscrowPanel.tsx:136-152`) and
waits for a manual tap. Add: a QR (same branded QR component as the funding
card) with a BIP21 `bitcoin:` URI carrying the exact amount; automatic polling
once shown; the brief-04 progress line (waiting → seen in mempool → confirmed);
and LOCK published automatically on confirmation, with the manual button kept
as a fallback. One confirmation stays the rule for now; say it on screen.

### 2d. Pay the winner directly

Today it's two transactions: escrow → per-trade key → sweep to the user's
wallet. Let the winner give a payout address (saved Lightning/on-chain
destinations already exist) and settle straight to it — one transaction, one
fee. Keep the per-trade key output as the fallback when no address is given.

### 2e. Cut the federation out of the on-chain path

The on-chain path still leans on Fedimint in places that fail exactly when a
federation does:

- the JOIN federation-ID gate still applies to on-chain trades
  (`useEscrow.ts:2280-2312`) — an on-chain trade must not require both people
  to share a federation;
- CREATE still carries `mintUrl`;
- the arbiter premium is paid from the Fedimint wallet after COMPLETED
  (`useEscrow.ts:~5479-5530`), skipped silently when there's no wallet.

Remove the gate and `mintUrl` requirement for on-chain trades. For the
premium: keep the current behaviour for now, but **say it** on the trade when
it couldn't be paid, instead of skipping silently. Paying it on-chain is Jet's
Taproot premium design (dispute leaf carries it; success path via a pre-signed
template) — next phase, its own brief.

### 2f. Fee bumping, minimum viable

Nothing can bump a stuck settlement. Minimum for this pass: the payout output
belongs to the winner, so a stuck settlement can be CPFP'd by spending that
output at a higher rate. With 2d that output is the winner's own wallet (their
wallet can bump it); without 2d, add a "speed up" that re-sweeps the per-trade
output at a higher rate. Don't fall back to the 2 sat/vB floor for settlement
when the fee API fails — refuse and retry.

## Part 3 — prove it

1. A **Mutinynet harness for the escrow** (none exists; the bond has one —
   model it on `scripts/mutinynet-commitment-harness.ts`). Build the script in
   this pass. If this environment can't reach Mutinynet or fund from its
   faucet, say so and leave exact run instructions for Jet instead of
   skipping it:
   COOP, DISPUTE with an auto-seated arbiter, REFUND after CLTV, and the 1b
   malicious-terms case rejected — all against real Mutinynet transactions,
   recorded with txids in the implementation note.
2. Then one small **mainnet** trade end to end, txids recorded.

## Order

Part 1 (a → d) first, as one release. Nothing in Part 2 ships before Part 1 —
making an unsafe path easier to reach is the wrong order. Then 2a–2c (reachable),
then 2d–2f (cheap and robust). Declare the transitive deps the code already
imports but package.json doesn't (`@scure/bip32`, `@scure/bip39`,
`@scure/base`, `@noble/curves`) in the first pass.
