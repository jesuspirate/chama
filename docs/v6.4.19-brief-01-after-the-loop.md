## A. Saved wallets: rename doesn't stick, deleted ones come back

Jet, on the APK: renamed the Lightning wallet in the claim picker
several times; the name reverts. A wallet he deleted reappeared on its
own. `payout-destinations.ts` is correct in isolation (rename maps and
writes; delete passes `allowEmptyOverwrite`), so the cause is around
it. Two suspects, check both with a log line before changing anything:

1. **Scoped key.** Storage is `chama_payout_destinations[:pubkey]`. If
   the rename runs before the signer's pubkey is known, it writes the
   unscoped key; the next read is scoped and the rename is gone.
   Log the key on every read/write once, on the Pixel, rename, kill,
   reopen.
2. **Legacy handle migration.** `migrateLegacyLightningHandles()` runs
   on every read and re-creates a destination for any handle still in
   `SAVED_HANDLES_STORAGE_KEY`. If the lock flow (`savedHandleId`, the
   trade-time handle reveal) still writes that store, every lock
   resurrects a deleted wallet and `dedupeDestinations` may prefer the
   unlabeled copy over the renamed one. Find every writer of the legacy
   key; the migration must run once and the legacy store must stay
   empty afterwards.

Fix whichever is true (both if both). Test: rename → relaunch → name
kept; delete → lock a trade with that handle → still deleted.

## H. The funder can't call the arbiter early (review, reader-first)

Jet: "the person waiting to be paid can call in the arbiter early, but
the person who put the money in can't." True, in every vertical, and
it's law, not UI:

- `firstHappyPathVoter`: the deed-doer votes first (Market: seller
  "I shipped"; Exchange/Bill Pay/Lending: buyer "I sent it / I paid
  it"). The other principal sees "waiting" and has no buttons.
- `oneSidedEscalationAt` (arbiter-substitution.ts): a standing
  one-sided RELEASE by the **non-locker** opens the arbiter's window
  after min(committed grace ≤ 4 h, half the remaining life). That is
  the payee's early door. The reducer enforces it (`ARBITER_TOO_EARLY`).
- The locker has no symmetric door. Their protection is expiry (live
  default 24 h; `trade-durations.ts` proposals unwired): after it,
  REFUND-only healing, unless a performance contest is standing.
- On-chain: the DISPUTE leaf is CSV 144 (~1 day) by script, so no one
  can bring the arbiter in earlier; REFUND CLTV 4320 (~30 days) is the
  no-arbiter exit; `SETTLEMENT_STALLED` 24 h covers a signer who
  approved and won't sign. Jet's reading is right.

Why the asymmetry is weaker than it looks: a locker's early REFUND
vote cannot move money alone; it needs a second vote. If the payee is
performing, they answer RELEASE (they get a notification now) and the
dispute is two-sided, arbiter rules on evidence. If the payee is
silent, the locker waits for expiry — which is the same wait the payee
gets on a silent locker *before* the escalation window was added.

Decision proposed: make escalation symmetric. A standing one-sided
REFUND by the **locker** opens the arbiter's window on the same
formula (`oneSidedEscalationAt` generalized to "any standing one-sided
principal vote"), and the silent side is woken: "Bestie asked the
arbiter to step in — respond within {time}." The arbiter still rules
on evidence; nothing is automatic. Expiry stays the backstop.

Sequencing, because this is reducer law: readers first. 6.4.19 ships
the reader (accept an arbiter vote inside the symmetric window) with
no UI door; the door ("Something's wrong? Ask the arbiter") ships once
the fleet has the reader, same cadence as the ring flags. Tests: both
one-sided arms open at the same offsets; a locker's REFUND + arbiter
REFUND resolves inside the window on new readers and is
`ARBITER_TOO_EARLY` on a pre-6.4.19 reader (document the thin view).
Lending keeps its own clock. On-chain unchanged.
