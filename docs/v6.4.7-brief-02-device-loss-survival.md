# Brief 02 — what survives a device loss, as a test (runway 19, step zero)

Runway 19 says it plainly: *"write down exactly what survives a device loss
TODAY (a test, not a doc claim), then (1), then (3). Do not promise portability
in copy until (1) is proven by an actual device-to-device restore."*

This brief is only step zero. It builds no portability feature. It produces the
evidence that tells us what we are allowed to say.

Author: Claude. Verified against `bb3d4091`.

## Why a test and not a paragraph

We have twice reasoned about custody from a surface instead of from a replay,
and twice got it wrong (see `docs/v6.4.7-brief-00-readiness-corrections.md`).
A paragraph claiming "your trades follow your key" cannot regress. A test can.

The shape is a **characterization test**: it records what actually happens
today, including the parts we do not like, and fails when that changes. It is
not a specification of what we want.

## What to build

New file `src/storage/device-loss.tests.ts`.

Construct a "cold device": a fresh, empty storage context holding **only** the
nsec, with no OPFS wallet, no user-scoped stores, no caches. Then assert, one
named assertion per artefact, what rehydrates and what does not.

The artefacts to cover, each with its own assertion and a comment saying why
the answer is what it is:

1. **The event chain.** Replay from relays with the nsec alone. Expect: fully
   recovered. This is the thing that genuinely is portable, and the test should
   prove it rather than assume it.
2. **Ecash notes in the OPFS wallet.** Bearer money bound to one browser
   profile. Expect: gone. Assert it is gone, and assert the UI-facing balance
   reads zero rather than stale.
3. **2-of-3 share material held by a locker.** Expect: gone with the profile.
   State explicitly what that means for an in-flight LOCKED trade where this
   device was the locker.
4. **The `pending-native-locks` stash.** Money-adjacent: it is what tells us
   whether sats were spent toward an unpublished lock. Expect: gone. This one
   matters most, because brief 00's advice ("check the stash before describing
   any sats as lost") silently stops working on a new device.
5. **The durable trade index** (`src/ui/latest-trade.ts` and the loss-proof
   history at `MeScreen.tsx:1345`). Expect: gone, but rebuildable from replay.
   Assert which of the two.
6. **`src/storage/` user-scoped stores** — `saved-nsec`, `identity-pin`,
   `forgotten-trades`, and the attention triage pins/snoozes. Low stakes, but
   enumerate them so the list is complete rather than representative.

For each, the assertion message must be a sentence a human can read in the test
output, e.g. `"cold device: ecash notes do NOT survive — bearer notes live in
this profile's OPFS wallet"`. The test output IS the document.

## Then write down what it found

Add a short section to `docs/v6.4-runway.md` under item 19, headed with the
date, stating the measured answer and pointing at the test as its source. Do
not restate the test in prose beyond a line per artefact — the test is
canonical and the doc points to it.

## Copy audit, same pass

Grep the i18n files for any string that promises recovery, portability, or
"your trades follow your key". Any string that over-promises against what the
test just measured gets corrected in all four languages (en/es/fr/sw). If a
string is accurate, leave it. List what you changed and what you left.

## What NOT to do

- Do not implement the wallet export/import hand-off (runway 19 item 1). That
  is a separate release and needs a real device-to-device restore to be called
  done.
- Do not soften an assertion because the answer is unflattering. An unflattering
  measured answer is the deliverable.
- Do not use a mock that makes recovery look better than it is. If the cold
  device cannot be constructed honestly in the test environment, say so and
  stop, rather than asserting against a fixture that assumes the answer.

## Verification

`npm test` with the new file. The suite should report the new assertions by
name; paste that block into the runway entry as the measurement record.
