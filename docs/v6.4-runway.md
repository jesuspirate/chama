# v6.4 runway — locked before Big Boss week (2026-09-07)

The three infrastructure items that ride alongside (or just ahead of) the
Chama vertical. Each exists because a real field failure demanded it.

## 1. Durable CLAIM publication (item zero — foundation SHIPPED in 6.3.3)

Field failures: the 30/100-sat zombie claims (CLAIM never reached a relay;
every fresh device re-summons the corpse) and the 2,000-sat cross-device
replay. 6.3.3 shipped the transport foundation: zombie-socket detection
(all-timeout signature → cycle pool → resend same signed event once),
stale-socket handler guards, and the preferred-relay republish queue already
existed. DONE (2026-09-09, pre-6.4): the durable
publish-until-acked queue for CLAIM landed. The signed event is persisted to
strict-scoped localStorage (durable-claim-queue.ts, mirroring the
pending-redemptions stash pattern) BEFORE the first publish attempt, and
retired only when the PREFERRED relay explicitly ACCEPTS it — tracked by a
new acceptance ledger in relay-manager (both the live-publish path and the
republish flush record accepts; "gone from the in-memory queue" was
ambiguous, since its 24h TTL and cap also remove entries). Drained after
each claim, 5s after boot, and on the 60s heartbeat. 30-day ceiling,
50-entry cap, corrupt storage degrades to an empty queue. 8 assertions in
the engine suite. A claim can now never stay redeemed-but-unpublished for
longer than connectivity itself — in place before the first weekly circle
completes (Sunday).

## 2. APK background alerts (FCM + UnifiedPush)

Field failure: "Background alerts" permanently disabled on the GrapheneOS
APK (Jet, 2026-09-07). Root cause is STRUCTURAL, not settings: the APK wraps
Android WebView, which has NO Web Push API on ANY Android — Googled or not.
The support matrix today:

- PWA installed from Chrome on Googled Android → web push WORKS
- APK on ANY Android → NO background alerts (WebView lacks PushManager)
- Degoogled devices (GrapheneOS etc.) → nothing works; even Chrome-family
  browser push rides FCM transport, absent without sandboxed Play Services
- iOS installed PWA → web push works (16.4+)

Fix: native push in the Capacitor shell — FCM for the Googled majority,
**UnifiedPush** for the degoogled crowd (no Google, distributor apps like
ntfy; exactly the audience Chama serves in TZ/KE). The VPS web-push watcher
already computes WHAT to notify; teach it to deliver to FCM and a
UnifiedPush endpoint alongside Web Push. Settings row should explain which
lane a device is on instead of a bare disabled toggle.

## 3. Chat image chunking (arbiter-grade evidence)

Field failure: 720px/18KB JPEG made screenshot evidence illegible (Jet,
2026-09-06). 6.3.2 bought legibility inside the single-event budget
(WebP-first, 960px, 26k chars), but true full-resolution evidence needs the
image split across multiple events and reassembled — the ecash QR-frames
pattern, applied to chat attachments. Design constraints: 3× NIP-44
per-participant encryption, 128KB silent relay cap, partial-delivery
rendering (show what arrived, mark what's missing).

## 4. The pre-lock clock — DONE (6.4)

Field failure (Jet, 2026-09-07, prod 6.3): *"the buyer is seeing the
countdown for the entire trade listing forever, which is incorrect if the
seller didn't lock in time. The trade silently goes back in the Browse and
the buyer is stuck waiting literally until the listing expires."*

Both countdowns in LiveTradeSurface rendered `state.expiresAt` — the
**listing** expiry — while a seated participant waited on the funder. But a
CREATED trade does not live that long: `getEffectiveParticipantAt` releases
a seat once `hold.expiresAt + JOIN_HOLD_LOCK_GRACE_SECONDS` passes, and the
listing quietly returns to Browse. The room kept counting down a week on a
trade that had been dead for minutes.

Fix: `preLockDeadline(state, nowSec)` in `ui/decisions.ts` — the earliest of
every live join hold's lapse (hold + hidden grace) and the listing expiry,
with a `lapsed` flag. A tranche plan freezes the seats, so there it is the
listing expiry again. Both countdown sites consume it; past the deadline the
room says the seats were released and nothing was locked, instead of
counting down. 10 assertions in the engine suite.

## 5. The room strip — DONE (6.4)

Jet, same pass: *"can we bring back our top price banner and online + name +
ready while we're trading? I think it's important."* — and the earlier side
note that the trade view doesn't show the participants at all. This is
PHILOSOPHY.md rule 1 (*trade with people, not platforms*) failing in the one
screen where it matters most: a room that showed a status enum and no person.

`tradeRoomPresence()` returns buyer + seller as people. Presence is
**evidence only** — the newest thing that pubkey actually published, across
the event chain and the trade chat. Under two minutes reads "here now",
under fifteen "just here", otherwise just "holds the seat"; an empty slot
says "open seat". We do not fake a presence protocol: a green dot that means
nothing is worse than no dot. `ready` is "nothing is waiting on them at this
status" — the funder owes the lock while CREATED (category-aware, so p2p
flips it), and once LOCKED it means they have voted. A future-dated stamp
from a skewed clock reads "just now", never a negative age.

The compact BTC price pill rides the same strip, so the number you are
trading against no longer vanishes the moment a trade opens. 11 assertions.

## 12. NIP-46 remote-signer login (queued 2026-09-17, post-6.5)

Advanced login without ever importing keys: connect to any NIP-46 signer
(bunker:// or nostrconnect:// QR — Primal's apps already serve as one from
any device). Request the full kind-scoped grant ONCE at connect
(sign_event for the escrow kinds + nip44_encrypt/decrypt), so everything
auto-approves after the handshake — no per-event tapping. The one real
engineering task: Chama's decrypt volume (a circle open decrypts dozens
of events, each NIP-46 call a relay round-trip) demands a persistent
decrypted-payload cache — each event decrypted once EVER, not once per
screen. Libraries exist (Welshman nip-46 signer, applesauce-signers):
integration work, not protocol work. Flags: a bunker-backed arbiter heals
only while their bunker is reachable (their latency record will say so
honestly); watcher votes and sim mode need grant coverage. Pairs with the
arbiter roster — a public-figure arbiter keeping their nsec in Primal
while arbitrating from any device is exactly the recruiting demographic.
Research note alongside: NIP-06 mnemonic derivation is official; nsec-
tree-style unlinkable sub-identities (one master, one identity per
community) is the privacy shape worth watching.

## 17. Notifications we can stand behind — UnifiedPush + the quiet-sign-in law (Jet, 2026-09-19 — hard mark)

Jet: *"everyone relies on notifications and we need to nail them hard on first
try... I still get notifications that are random when I didn't do anything,
just signed in."* Two jobs, one lane — do NOT delay this behind 6.5.

**(a) The random-buzz-on-sign-in bug.** `notifyLiveSinceRef` is set when the
HOOK MOUNTS (page load), not when hydration settles, and the freshness gate
only asks whether the state's newest SIGNED moment is at or after that mark.
So anything whose signed timestamp lands in the hydration window can buzz even
though the user did nothing. Three specific suspects, in likelihood order:

1. The pre-lock on-chain ARBITER branch in `notificationForTransition` is the
   one path that deliberately fires with no `prev` ("freshness-gated first
   observation"). Jet is in `BLF_OFFICIAL_ARBITERS`, so every on-chain trade
   whose buyer joined recently can summon HIM on sign-in for a trade he never
   touched. Needs an explicit "this pool arbiter is actually seated / opted in
   to this trade" gate, not merely pool membership.
2. `maybeNotifyNewListing` — opted-in home-chama listings published during the
   hydration window read as "new" because they genuinely are; correct, but it
   lands in the same burst and feels random.
3. `maybeNotifyBuyerInterest` on a JOIN hold observed fresh during hydration.

THE LAW to implement: a QUIET WINDOW. Nothing buzzes until hydration has
settled (or N seconds after connect), and inside that window only a moment
that is BOTH newer than connect AND on a trade the viewer is a seated party to
may fire. Everything else is history, however freshly it arrived. Keep the
`notifyDebug` trail so a field report names its own cause.

**(b) UnifiedPush + FCM (runway item 2, promoted).** Closed-app delivery on the
APK: UnifiedPush for the de-Googled builds (Jet's GrapheneOS), FCM where Play
services exist, the VPS wake path already sketched in item 2. Pair with (a):
a push that arrives when nothing happened is worse than no push at all, so the
quiet-sign-in law ships first or alongside — never after.

## 18. The arbiter presence question (Jet, 2026-09-19 — open, think before building)

Jet: *"is it a silly idea for arbiters to do a 'kyc dance of some sort' ONLY
during high value trades + bond? ... a proof of life mechanism only for the
arbiter to make the buyer and seller content with a non-trusted party?"*

Not silly — but name what it can and cannot do. Cryptography cannot prove two
pubkeys are different PEOPLE; it can only prove one party controls something
another party cannot cheaply obtain. So the question is never "is this arbiter
a real person" but "what did this arbiter put at risk, and what would collusion
with one side cost them". That is the bond, and the bond is already the answer
Chama chose. A liveness ritual (a signed challenge, a timed response, a video)
is forgeable by exactly the adversary we care about — the counterparty running
both seats — and adds a trust ceremony that implies a guarantee it cannot give.

What actually moves the needle, in order:

1. **Cost of identity, not proof of identity.** Bond size scaled to trade size
   (already the design), plus the arbiter ROSTER's public record: healings
   served, median latency, disputes voted, bond history. A sybil can be minted;
   a record cannot be back-dated.
2. **Choice before involvement** — Jet's actual question. Buyer and seller
   should pick from a roster they can READ before a trade starts, with the
   record visible at selection time, not meet a random pubkey at dispute time.
   That is the arbiter-roster spec, and it is the highest-value work here.
3. **Structural separation beats attestation.** Deterministic arbiter selection
   from a bonded pool, seeded by the trade id, so neither side chooses who
   arbitrates their own dispute; plus the substitution ladder already built.
4. A LIVENESS SIGNAL (are they reachable right now) is honest and useful — and
   Chama already has it. Keep it as a signal, never dress it as identity.

Deliberately NOT doing: any KYC dance, any document, any video ritual. It would
be the only custodial-feeling thing in a non-custodial app, and it would be the
easiest thing in the system to fake.

## 19. Portable, recoverable active Chama trades (Jet, 2026-09-19 — open)

*"How do we solve: make active Chama trades portable/recoverable?"*

What is already portable: the CHAIN. Every escrow is Nostr events; any device
with the nsec rebuilds the trade by replay (that is what discovery + loadEscrow
already do). What is NOT portable is the ecash — bearer notes in THIS device's
OPFS wallet — and the 2-of-3 share material a locker holds.

So the real problem statement: a locked share is bearer money bound to one
browser profile. The three candidate shapes, cheapest first:

1. **Recovery-key export/import of the OPFS wallet** (exists in part today via
   the seed): make it a first-class "move this trade to my other device" flow
   with an explicit hand-off so the old device can no longer double-spend it.
2. **Fedimint-side recovery** — the federation already knows the notes; a
   client restored from seed can re-derive. Verify what survives a wipe today
   and write that down honestly before promising it.
3. **6.5.1 headless node healer** (runway 10): the node holds the durable
   copy, so a browser is only ever a view. This is the real answer for Start9
   users and the one that makes "portable" true rather than "re-syncable".

Order of work: write down exactly what survives a device loss TODAY (a test,
not a doc claim), then (1), then (3). Do not promise portability in copy until
(1) is proven by an actual device-to-device restore.

## 24. DONE (6.4.5) — ghost trades: two different lies, both fixed

Jet, 2026-09-19: *"I caught ghost trades again... the amount displayed in
orange even doubled from 21 to 42 magically."* Two unrelated causes:

**(a) The doubling was the host counting everyone's seat.** In a circle the
host is counterparty (SELLER) on EVERY member's share, and
`sumActiveBuyerSellerTradeMsats` summed every trade the viewer is a party to
— so a 21-sat seat read as 42 the moment a second member locked, and would
have read 84 at four. But the host's money at risk is their OWN seat. Fixed:
a circle share counts toward "my active trades" only for the member sitting
in it (`isSomeoneElsesCircleSeat`, tested). The pot's fate is the circle's
story, told on the circle screen, not in the viewer's exposure headline.

**(b) The READY queue ghosts are unheld buyer seats.** `getEffectiveParticipantAt`
only expires a seat it can find a JOIN HOLD for: `if (!hold ...) return pubkey`.
So a CREATED listing whose buyer seat has no recorded reservation (a legacy
JOIN, or any path that seated a buyer without writing a hold) pins the listing
in the seller's READY queue FOREVER — "order finalized · waiting for your lock"
on a buyer who left months ago. Fixed at the display layer (no protocol
change): a seat with no hold ages out 24h after its JOIN (or the listing's
creation when no JOIN is in view) and the listing returns to STOCK, where it
can be edited or deleted. A signed tranche plan still freezes seats on purpose
and is exempt.

Watch next: if unheld seats keep appearing on NEW trades, the write path that
seats a buyer without a hold is the real bug — this guard would then be hiding
it rather than cleaning up after it. Worth a targeted check during 6.5.

## 32. OPEN — a republished chain the other side still cannot see (Jet, 2026-09-20)

The sharpest evidence yet, and it is NOT the claim path:

- Seller (Jetty) opens `sm_mtnxzb5y_zpepqcpj`, taps HEAL THIS TRADE, gets
  **"Re-published 6/6 events to today's relays."** The trade reads SETTLING —
  "Refunded back to the buyer. Nothing left to do here."
- Buyer (Bestie), same minute, pastes that ID into Load-a-trade and gets
  **"Trade not found on relays."** Repeatedly, across refreshes.

So one client says it wrote six events and the other cannot read one. Only a
few things explain that, and they are worth separating before guessing again:

1. **Different relay sets.** The two browsers are different origins with their
   own storage; if their effective relay pools diverge (backoff having retired
   relays on one side, a different DEFAULT_RELAYS in one build), "published"
   and "queried" are simply different places. FIRST thing to check: the relay
   list and connected count on BOTH sides at the same moment.
2. **Accepted but not stored.** A relay may ACK a write and still not serve it
   back — free relays evict aggressively, and escrow kinds (38100–38108) sit
   in the ADDRESSABLE range, where a relay keeps only the newest event per
   (pubkey, kind, d-tag). Any two events sharing that triple silently replace
   each other. This needs verifying against a real relay with a real chain; if
   true, it is the root cause of every "the relays dropped that part" symptom
   in this log, including the LOCK body.
3. **Query-shape mismatch**: republish writes with one tag shape, the reader
   filters on another.

Shipped meanwhile (diagnosis, not a fix): Load-a-trade now passes
`repairFromCache` and NAMES the failure instead of saying "not found" for all
three cases — relays empty vs undecryptable vs chain-incomplete (+ replay
code). That distinction is what tells us which of the three above is true, and
it costs one paste to find out.

ANSWERED, 2026-09-20 02:30 — one paste settled it. With the named failure in
place, Bestie now says:

  "This trade's history couldn't be rebuilt on this device, so the summary
   here is what's saved locally. It may rebuild later, or on another device
   that still holds the full chain. (INVALID_STATE)"

So the events ARE arriving. Delivery was never the problem; the relays are
fine and the republish worked. The chain will not REPLAY on the buyer's side:
one event is being applied to a state that does not accept it
(`state-machine.ts` — "Cannot JOIN/LOCK/VOTE/RESOLVE/CLAIM in state X").

Start here in the morning, in this order:

1. **Which event and which state.** `LoadFailure.eventId` already carries the
   offending event id; surface it next to the code (it is in the struct, just
   not in the copy). That names the exact transition in one read instead of a
   hunt.
2. **The likely shape**: this trade has five signed steps (created, joined,
   voted, voted, resolved) and the SELLER just re-broadcast all six events. A
   duplicate or out-of-order VOTE/RESOLVE landing after the state already
   moved is exactly what raises INVALID_STATE — and `useEscrow` already notes
   that duplicates across clients are expected and "deduped at replay via
   ALREADY_VOTED / TERMINAL_STATE / INVALID_STATE".
3. **The design question underneath, which is the real prize**: a replay that
   ABANDONS the whole history because one late/duplicate event does not fit is
   fragile by construction. Duplicates are normal in a relay world — that is
   why the dedup codes exist. A non-money-bearing event that cannot apply
   should be SKIPPED with a recorded note, not allowed to invalidate a rebuild
   that is otherwise complete and provable. Money-bearing transitions (LOCK,
   CLAIM) must stay strict. Getting this rule right probably fixes a whole
   family of "couldn't rebuild on this device" reports, not just this trade.

Also spotted in the same screenshots, unrelated and small: the trade room
says "Resolved — funds refunded to the seller" (`lts.resolvedRefunded`,
LiveTradeSurface) while the details panel says "Refunded back to the buyer"
(`trade.nsRefundedBackTo`, party-aware). On a goods sale the buyer locks, so
REFUND returns to the BUYER and the LiveTradeSurface line is simply wrong —
it hardcodes "seller" where the other surface computes the party. Fix with
the party-aware string.

Run `node --import tsx scripts/probe-escrow-retention.ts` (optional relay URLs).
