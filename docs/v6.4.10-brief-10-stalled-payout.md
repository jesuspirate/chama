## The gap

On an on-chain exchange the seller funds. After the buyer pays fiat and
the seller confirms (RELEASE vote → APPROVED), the payout still needs the
seller's Bitcoin signature. Nothing forces it. The reducer refuses an
arbiter vote once both agree (`state-machine.ts:1275`, "Arbiter vote not
needed"), and the settlement code lets the arbiter co-sign only when the
arbiter was part of the ruling. A seller who confirms and then goes quiet
keeps the sats when the 30-day refund matures — with the buyer's fiat.

## A. Confirming is signing

The seller's "I received the payment" and the seller's payout signature
become one action. Order of play on an on-chain trade:

1. Buyer marks fiat as sent **and chooses the destination** in the same
   step (address, or "my Chama key"). This publishes the winner-authored
   unsigned payout (today's `prepareOnchainSettlement`), before approval.
2. Seller's confirm builds the same transaction locally, verifies it (right
   inputs, right destination, right amount), signs it, and publishes the
   RELEASE vote and the signed settlement together. If signing fails, the
   confirm does not go out; say why.
3. Buyer's device sees APPROVED + a seller-signed payout, adds its own
   signature, broadcasts. No further tap unless something failed.

The reverse case (buyer as the non-winner on marketplace/bill flows where
the buyer funds) mirrors this: whoever votes to release the other side's
win signs in the same act. Test: on an on-chain trade, a RELEASE vote from
the funder never exists without a matching signed settlement from the
same key, same event batch.

## B. Fallback: the arbiter finishes a stalled payout

For trades approved by older builds, or if A's publish half-failed:

1. Reducer: on an on-chain trade in APPROVED where a winner-authored
   payout exists and the other party's signature is absent 24 h after the
   approval (`approvedAt + 86_400`), the arbiter may co-sign a
   dispute-leaf payout to the winner's chosen destination. New transition
   `SETTLEMENT_STALLED` (winner publishes it after 24 h; it is a request,
   not a ruling) → arbiter's bar shows needs-you → arbiter signs → winner
   finalizes. The arbiter checks nothing but the three facts: RELEASE vote
   by the other party, winner-authored PSBT, no counter-signature.
2. Winner's screen from the moment of approval: "If Jetty doesn't sign
   within 24 h you can ask the arbiter" with a countdown, then the button.
3. Deadline copy for the winner throughout: "This must settle before
   <date> (block N); after that the sats can go back to the funder."
   Derived from the committed refund height.

## C. The mark

When an escrow is spent through the arbitrated leaf on a trade the
non-winner had approved (verifiable: their RELEASE vote + the on-chain
spend's leaf), that key carries a permanent, public mark per the skill:
"Made a buyer wait for the arbiter after agreeing to pay — N time(s)". It
shows on every listing card, offer card, trade room seat chip and arbiter-
style record for that key, in red, ahead of any other fact. No expiry,
no appeal, no composite hiding it. Pure function from (events, chain
reads); tested with two event orderings.

## D. Standing, first two facts

Not the whole system — the first two facts that already have verifiable
sources today, shown on listing cards and seat chips per the skill:

- "Signs within <median> (of N)" — for sellers: median time from a
  buyer's "paid" event to the seller's signed settlement; for arbiters:
  dispute opened → ruling. Needs ≥3 samples, else omitted.
- "Bonded <sats> for <days>" / "N trades settled on chain".
