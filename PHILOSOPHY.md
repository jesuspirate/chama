# Trade with people, not platforms

The landing page says it. This file is how we hold ourselves to it.

Every screen in Chama answers one question before any other: **is there a
human on the other side of this, and can I see them?** A platform hides the
counterparty and asks you to trust the brand. We do the opposite: the brand
is nothing, the people are everything, and the software's only job is to
make sure neither of you needs to trust the other.

## The four rules

1. **Name the human.** Nobody should ever be shown a bare pubkey where a
   person belongs. Every key resolves to a name — their published one if
   they have it, a generated Adjective-Animal otherwise (Swift Twiga,
   Golden Simba), identical on every device with zero setup. Hex is for
   verification, never for identity. If a surface shows a counterparty, it
   shows their name, and the trade room shows WHO IS IN THE ROOM.
2. **Name the role, not the machinery.** "Buyer", "seller", "the person who
   locks" — never "the initiator of the 38102 event". Protocol vocabulary
   belongs in the code and the docs; the screens speak about people doing
   things to each other's money.
3. **Every human role is discoverable.** A feature nobody can find does not
   exist. If a merchant can opt out of arbitrating, they must be able to
   SEE that arbitrating was on offer and what it would have required. If
   arbiters are the backbone of dispute safety, the path to becoming one is
   advertised, not buried behind a toggle nobody scrolls to. The community
   cannot staff itself from a secret.
4. **Say who is watching your money, and where.** An arbiter bonded for
   months should be able to see, at a glance, which community they are
   serving. A member locking sats should see the names of everyone whose
   round they are joining. Long-lived commitments need standing answers to
   "who, and where" — not a memory test.

## Why Chama (the vertical) is the proof

The savings circle is this philosophy with the volume turned up. There is no
platform in it anywhere: no treasurer holding the pot, no operator taking a
cut, no company promising a price. There is a group of named people, a
deadline everyone agreed to before a single sat moved, and arithmetic that
returns everyone's exact sats if the group doesn't gather.

The tiers say it in the language itself — you arrive as **Mgeni**, a guest,
and you earn your way to **Mwenyeji**, a host who receives guests. That is a
relationship between people, not a subscription tier.

Even the referee is built to be humanly minimal: a chama share's arbiter
cannot steal, cannot judge, and cannot block. They hold a key so that
mathematics can finish what people started.

The merry-go-round — rotating collection, arriving in v6.5 — keeps the
volume up. The pot pays exactly one member each round, chosen by
arithmetic: whoever locked fastest among those still owed, with the host
always last. There is no event in the protocol that expresses "I yield my
turn," so the argument about privilege cannot be had. And when someone
collects and then abandons the circle, no committee convenes: their
standing zeroes by computation and the chain remembers the date. Memory,
not judgment.

## The test, before you ship any screen

- Does it show a person where a person exists, by name?
- Would a stranger discover the roles this community needs filled?
- Does it explain what the OTHER human must do next, in their words?
- Does it promise anything a platform would have to be trusted for?
  If yes: delete the promise, not the trust — there should be nothing to
  trust in the first place.
